\section{Extended Methods}
\label{app:methods}

\subsection{Geometry construction and susceptibility endpoint}
\label{app:geometry}

Within each checkpoint, the analysis uses the frozen target-token intervention
coordinate and the predeclared within-layer strong-channel mask. The mask is
constructed independently at each checkpoint using the same frozen rule rather
than transferring channel identities across scales. For each of XG2 and XG4,
frozen direction rows are normalized to unit norm. Their float64 CPU uncentered
second moment is eigendecomposed and the five largest-eigenvalue eigenvectors
define the corresponding top-five local subspace. The frozen
projector-contrast construction pairs the two subspaces into five
checkpoint-local principal planes; sign canonicalization and orthonormality
rules are deterministic. No XG1 response is used to reconstruct, rotate, or
select these bases.

For a unit probe direction \(w\), the directional susceptibility is
\[
J_i(w)=\frac{F_i(+\epsilon;w)-F_i(-\epsilon;w)}{2\epsilon},
\qquad \epsilon=0.025.
\]
For the 130M projector-contrast analysis, plane \(k\), with frozen
projector-separation weight \(s_k\), contributes
\[
C_k(i)=\frac{s_k}{5}\left[J_i(\mathrm{PP}_{k+})^2-J_i(\mathrm{PP}_{k-})^2\right].
\]
The broader five-dimensional endpoint used for necessity and restoration is
\[
E_{\mathrm{XG2}}(i,c)=\frac{1}{5}\sum_j J_i(v_{\mathrm{XG2},j};c)^2,
\quad
E_{\mathrm{XG4}}(i,c)=\frac{1}{5}\sum_j J_i(v_{\mathrm{XG4},j};c)^2,
\]
\[
Q_i(c)=E_{\mathrm{XG2}}(i,c)-E_{\mathrm{XG4}}(i,c).
\]
At 370M and 1.4B, the same structural procedure is applied independently;
plane vectors, selected rank, response-blind control rank, strong-channel
indices, residual sign pattern, and coefficient-mass allocation are not
transferred between checkpoints.

\subsection{Prospective 130M validation}
\label{app:130m}

Static localization identified PP3 as the dominant shared positive
projector-contrast candidate before prospective XG1 validation. External
transport was evaluated on a fresh 300-pair XG1 population. Specificity used a
second disjoint 300-pair population and the response-blind hard geometric
control
\[
\mathrm{PP5}=\arg\max_k \sin(\theta_k),
\]
selected from frozen projector separation without XG1 responses. PP5 is not a
random baseline.

Necessity used a third fresh 300-pair population. Let \(h\) be the native
strong-coordinate target state and \(u_{3+},u_{3-}\) the orthonormal PP3 modes,
with native coefficients \(a=\langle h,u_{3+}\rangle\) and
\(b=\langle h,u_{3-}\rangle\). PP3 neutralization is
\[
T_3(h)=h-a u_{3+}-b u_{3-}.
\]
The matched PP5 control transfers the same coefficients into PP5,
\[
T_5(h)=h-a u_{5+}-b u_{5-}.
\]
Because both planes use orthonormal bases and the same coefficient pair, the
correction magnitudes are matched. The primary necessity contrast is evaluated
with the broader \(Q\) endpoint rather than a PP3-specific response measure.
It establishes local necessity relative to the matched control, not global or
downstream behavioral necessity.

Restoration used a fourth disjoint 300-pair XG1 population. From the
PP3-neutralized background
\[
B=h-(a u_{3+}+b u_{3-}),
\]
selected restoration returns the exact removed component,
\[
R_3=B+a u_{3+}+b u_{3-}=h,
\]
while the matched replacement is
\[
R_5=B+a u_{5+}+b u_{5-}.
\]
The additions have equal Euclidean norm. This is a local matched
restoration/replacement test, not sufficiency in an empty state or sufficiency
for downstream task behavior. Finite-\(\epsilon\) robustness at 0.025, 0.0125,
and 0.00625 is descriptive and is not extrapolated to an
\(\epsilon\rightarrow0\) theorem.

\subsection{Cross-checkpoint recurrence and residual characterization}
\label{app:recurrence}

For each larger checkpoint, discovery defines a scale-local candidate using
\[
G_{s,k}(i)=Q_{\mathrm{restored}}(s,k,i)-Q_{\mathrm{neutralized}}(s,k,i)
\]
and freezes the unique mean maximizer before confirmation. A response-blind
control is chosen from the remaining frozen geometry without using the
confirmation outcome. The confirmation endpoint is
\[
D_{\mathrm{CORE},s}(i)=Q_{\mathrm{restored}}(s,k_s^*,i)-Q_{\mathrm{control}}(s,c_s^*,i).
\]
This protocol tests recurrence of a dominant causal \emph{role}; it does not
require the selected plane rank to match across checkpoints.

All non-selected planes form the residual mechanism for descriptive
characterization. Signed residual effects, coefficient-mass allocation,
dominant residual rank, and generator-family coupling are reported without a
post-hoc cross-checkpoint residual significance test. At 370M, the residual
coefficient-mass shares are approximately P1 0.064, P2 0.148, P4 0.094, and P5
0.695; the signed effects include a large negative P5 and positive P4 term. At
1.4B, the residual mass is concentrated primarily on P2 (about 0.574) and P3
(about 0.318), with negative P2/P4 and a smaller positive P3 contribution.
Within-checkpoint aggregate residuals closely track the signed sum of residual
planes (correlations 0.963 and 0.995 at 370M and 1.4B). These values motivate
the descriptive ``residual-plastic'' label but do not define a scaling law.

\subsection{Adjacent-site specificity and cross-block transport}
\label{app:transport}

The Mamba-1.4B canonical source/target/intervention mapping is
\((33,34,35)\). The adjacent site is fixed by shifting the triplet exactly one
block downstream to \((34,35,36)\), preserving relative offsets. The choice is
architecture/instrumentation based and is not reopened after response
observation. Only this one adjacent site is used; the experiment does not
identify a global layer optimum.

The adjacent geometry is reconstructed independently from the same
response-free XG2/XG4 structural cohorts. The same-numbered adjacent P5 is a
rank-aligned site-local plane, not a semantic identity claim. The adjacent
response-blind control is selected by the frozen geometry-only rule and is P4.

For geometric transport, the canonical P5 rank-two subspace is propagated
through the intervening block with the frozen local Jacobian-vector transport
procedure. On 600 response-free rows the transported subspace remains rank two.
Principal angles and projector overlap quantify its relation to the independently
reconstructed adjacent P5 subspace. No response threshold or inferential
classification is produced by this geometry runner.

The response experiment reuses the fixed 300-pair adjacent population. The
primary paired endpoint is
\[
G=D_{\mathrm{TRANSPORT}}-D_{\mathrm{ADJ}},
\]
with exactly one one-sided Student t test. The separate sign gate
\(\mathrm{mean}(D_{\mathrm{TRANSPORT}})>0\) is mandatory. The observed relative
shift is positive and strongly supported, while the transported mean remains
negative; therefore positive adjacent-response restoration is not established.
Ratios between canonical, adjacent, and transported means are not interpreted
as mediation or explained percentages.

\subsection{Downstream readout, gradient ownership, and behavior}
\label{app:readout}

The matched readout population is \(\mathrm{xg1\_fact\_4801..5100}\),
\(N=300\) source pairs, with the frozen C0\_SHAM and C2\_NAME cells averaged
within pair. For correct class \(y\), native downstream task margin is
\[
m=z_y-\max_{c\neq y}z_c.
\]
The upstream graph is cut at the frozen intervention tensor, treated as a local
differentiation leaf; downstream computation remains intact and parameters
receive no gradients. If \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\) denote
the frozen selected and coefficient-matched control components, the row-level
readout is
\[
\Delta L_{\mathrm{row}}=g^\top(C_{\mathrm{sel}}-C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}),
\]
then averaged within pair. The primary paired endpoint is
\[
R_q=\Delta L_{370\mathrm{M},q}-\Delta L_{1.4\mathrm{B},q}.
\]

The historical model uses edge-specific gradient ownership. For the frozen
\texttt{G3-GROUP-D-HALF} arm, all relevant routes into the final decision pass
through a D edge with ownership factor 0.5. The forward value is unchanged but
the autograd sensitivity is halved. Consequently
\[
\Delta L_{\mathrm{owned}}=0.5\,\Delta L_{\mathrm{forward}},\qquad
\Delta L_{\mathrm{forward}}=2\,\Delta L_{\mathrm{owned}}.
\]
This source-defined factor explains the earlier approximately two-fold
finite-forward calibration and is not a Mamba numerical anomaly. Because it is
a common positive factor, it does not alter signs, rankings, t statistics, or
p-values.

Direct behavioral coupling uses the same selected-versus-control component
construction. For stagewise localization, downstream margins are recorded
under native, dominant-neutralized, and dominant-control conditions after the
frozen intervention. At each stage \(k\),
\[
A_{\mathrm{sel}}(k)=M_{\mathrm{native}}(k)-M_{\mathrm{neutralized}}(k),
\]
\[
B_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(k)=M_{\mathrm{control}}(k)-M_{\mathrm{neutralized}}(k),
\]
\[
D(k)=M_{\mathrm{native}}(k)-M_{\mathrm{control}}(k)
=A_{\mathrm{sel}}(k)-B_{\mathrm{ctrl}}(k).
\]
Stagewise quantities are descriptive and do not acquire additional p-values or
a unique-mediator interpretation.

\subsection{AVeriTeC transfer and steering boundary}
\label{app:averitec}

External transfer uses the official AVeriTeC development source with annotated
gold evidence. The compatible three-class cohort excludes the
\texttt{Conflicting Evidence/Cherrypicking} class and contains 462 examples,
with the frozen mapping Refuted\(\rightarrow\)REFUTE, Not Enough
Evidence\(\rightarrow\)NOT\_ENTITLED, and Supported\(\rightarrow\)SUPPORT.
No evidence retrieval, question generation, or justification-field input is
used. Claim text and gold evidence are serialized deterministically under the
model-specific frozen tokenizer/anchor contract; no favorable subset is
selected after tokenization or response observation.

For each example, the causal comparison uses native, dominant-neutralized, and
coefficient-matched dominant-control conditions and measures the correct-class
margin. The completed 130M/370M tests form their frozen two-test external
transfer family. The later 1.4B negative-sign experiment is a separate
prospective extension and does not reopen that family. At 1.4B the point
estimate is negative, but the one-sided less-than test has \(p=0.0757\), so
negative external transfer is not established.

Prediction-level control is tested separately at 370M with one preregistered
fixed-mirror steering rule on 2,799 examples. There are 331 natively correct
and 2,468 natively incorrect examples, with zero wrong-to-correct transitions
and zero correct-to-wrong transitions. Because there are no discordant pairs,
the pre-specified exact utility test is not estimable. This result bounds that
fixed steering rule only and does not establish general unsteerability.

\subsection{Statistical scope and provenance}
\label{app:statistics}

Prospective causal contrasts use the test direction and endpoint frozen before
response inspection. Positive mean contrasts are generally evaluated with
one-sided Student t tests on the predeclared item or paired-difference endpoint.
Multiplicity correction is applied only where a prospective family explicitly
defines multiple primary tests, including the frozen two-test Holm families.
Residual decompositions, finite-\(\epsilon\) profiles, geometric transport
summaries, and stagewise localization remain descriptive and are not assigned
post-hoc p-values. Failed gates are not rescued by changing population, plane,
layer, token, epsilon, control, or test direction.

Every scientific stage freezes cohort, model/checkpoint, tokenizer/anchor,
geometry/intervention object, endpoint, and artifact identity before downstream
interpretation. Raw execution artifacts are separated from static inferential
analyses and manuscript synthesis. The manuscript figures and table are
generated from frozen artifacts; manuscript construction performs no model
execution or new statistical testing.

\section{Additional Result Details}
\label{app:results}

\subsection{130M validation statistics}

The external PP3 transport mean is \(7.1203\times10^{-8}\) with SD
\(6.4998\times10^{-8}\). In the fresh specificity study, PP3 mean is
\(7.1235\times10^{-8}\) and PP5 mean is \(4.8194\times10^{-8}\), yielding
\(D_{\mathrm{SPEC}}=2.3041\times10^{-8}\). The matched necessity contrast is
\(4.7414\times10^{-8}\) with SD \(3.4242\times10^{-8}\); the matched
restoration contrast is \(4.0789\times10^{-8}\) with SD
\(3.5139\times10^{-8}\). These studies use four non-overlapping fresh
populations and do not adaptively reuse outcomes for later selection.

\subsection{Cross-checkpoint readout and behavioral context}

For the primary matched readout comparison, ownership-weighted means are
\(+0.0005089564\) at 370M and \(-0.0011954475\) at 1.4B, with paired mean
difference \(+0.0017044039\) and paired SD \(0.0027407135\). The common
forward-equivalent factor of two yields means \(+0.0010179\) and
\(-0.0023909\) without changing inference. The separately frozen 130M
ownership-weighted contextual readout is \(+0.0011203660\), with
forward-equivalent \(+0.0022407321\); it is not included in the matched
370M--1.4B test.

On direct behavior, 370M has mean \(D_{\mathrm{BEH}}=+0.001120686\), while
1.4B has mean \(-0.002012885\). The pre-specified positive-bridge family
therefore supports the 370M bridge but not the same positive claim at 1.4B.
The stagewise analysis first shows persistent C2 opposition at
\texttt{post\_block\_35}; aggregate pair-level opposition becomes persistent at
\texttt{post\_block\_47}. These stages are descriptive localization, not an
inferential mediator decomposition.

\section{Expanded Limitations}
\label{app:limitations}

\paragraph{Checkpoint and seed scope.}
The study analyzes three fixed pretrained checkpoints and does not estimate
variation over independently trained model seeds. The matched 370M--1.4B test
therefore supports an item-level fixed-checkpoint contrast, not a model-population
scaling law or phase transition.

\paragraph{Architecture scope.}
All primary evidence is within the tested Mamba family. The results do not
establish architecture-independent universality or imply that the same
role/geometry/readout separation must occur in Transformers or other SSMs.

\paragraph{Procedure-defined geometry.}
Principal planes are checkpoint-local objects induced by the frozen XG2/XG4
reconstruction, strong-channel rule, target coordinate, and finite-difference
measurement. Recurring function does not imply same-numbered-plane identity.

\paragraph{Residual reorganization.}
Cross-checkpoint residual changes are descriptive. No omnibus or pairwise
cross-scale residual significance test was added after observing the profiles.

\paragraph{Transport completeness.}
The 1.4B transport test supports a relative causal shift but fails the positive
restoration gate. It therefore does not identify a complete mediator and does
not justify an explained-percentage interpretation.

\paragraph{Site specificity.}
The site-specificity experiment compares the canonical site with one
prospectively fixed adjacent \(+1\) site. It does not establish uniqueness over
all layers or a global intervention optimum.

\paragraph{Finite perturbation.}
The susceptibility program uses primary \(\epsilon=0.025\) and smaller finite
values only for descriptive robustness. Stability over these values does not
prove an exact infinitesimal limit.

\paragraph{External validity.}
AVeriTeC uses the compatible three-class gold-evidence subset and removes
retrieval from the causal question. It is not an end-to-end benchmark or
retrieval evaluation. The 1.4B negative point estimate does not pass its
pre-specified alpha gate.

\paragraph{Steering scope.}
The steering null concerns one preregistered fixed-mirror transform at 370M. It
does not evaluate adaptive, optimization-based, or alternative-magnitude
control and therefore does not establish general unsteerability.
