\title{Causal Role Recurrence with Geometric Reorganization and Readout Reversal in Mamba}
\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Mechanistic analyses often ask whether an internal causal computation recurs
across model checkpoints, but recurrence does not specify what else is
preserved. We separate three properties---causal-role recurrence, geometric
realization, and downstream functional alignment---in frozen pretrained Mamba
checkpoints at 130M, 370M, and 1.4B parameters. A scale-local dominant causal
role recurs across the tested checkpoints, while its checkpoint-local
geometric realization reorganizes. At Mamba-1.4B, transporting the canonical
subspace across an adjacent block shifts the intervention response toward the
canonical effect but does not restore its positive response, showing that the
reorientation is functionally relevant but incomplete. Downstream alignment
also changes. In a prospectively matched comparison over 300 source pairs, the
independently frozen selected-versus-control displacement has positive mean
task-readout alignment at Mamba-370M and negative mean alignment at Mamba-1.4B,
supporting an orientation reversal despite recurrence of the internal causal
role. Direct intervention establishes a positive downstream behavioral bridge
at 370M while the same pre-specified positive bridge is not established at
1.4B. Together, these results show that, across the tested Mamba checkpoints,
recurrence of a causal role does not guarantee either a fixed geometric
realization or stable downstream functional alignment. Mechanistic
correspondences and intervention directions should therefore be revalidated
after checkpoint or scale changes rather than transferred from role recurrence
alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Mechanistic analysis often asks whether an internal computation identified in
one model also appears in another. A stricter question is what must remain
invariant when a causal role reappears. Recurrence could preserve the role
while changing the coordinates that realize it, the downstream task alignment
of the corresponding intervention, both, or neither. These possibilities are
usually conflated when mechanisms are transferred across checkpoints or model
scales.

Mamba offers a useful test bed. Selective state-space models replace attention
with input-dependent state-space dynamics \citep{gu2023mamba}, and prior work
has already established causal localization and editing
\citep{sharma2024locating}, token/layer information-flow interventions
\citep{endy2025knockout}, architecture-specific attribution
\citep{jafari2024mambalrp}, cross-architecture mechanistic similarity
\citep{wang2025universality,arora2025mechanistic}, and activation-subspace
interventions \citep{mohan2026subspace}. Causal localization can also be
non-unique and intervention-surface dependent \citep{jiang2026circuit}, while
mechanistic scaling has been studied in associative recall
\citep{koren2026recall}. We therefore do not ask whether Mamba contains
interpretable causal structure. We ask whether recurrence of a validated causal
role requires preservation of its geometry or downstream orientation.

We test these implications in frozen pretrained Mamba-130M, 370M, and 1.4B
checkpoints. Geometry is reconstructed independently within each checkpoint;
principal-plane rank is checkpoint-local and never treated as a semantic
identifier shared across models. The recurring object is functional: a
scale-local component that plays the dominant causal role relative to a frozen
response-blind control.

Our first result is \textbf{causal-role recurrence without fixed geometric
realization}. The starting 130M component is prospectively validated by
external-generator transport, response-blind specificity, matched-control
necessity and restoration, and finite-perturbation robustness. Applying the
same procedure at larger checkpoints again identifies a dominant causal role,
but the selected rank and surrounding residual organization change. We call
this bounded pattern \textbf{core-stable / residual-plastic}. At 1.4B,
cross-block transport additionally shows that the geometric reorientation is
functionally relevant while failing to restore the canonical positive effect.

Our second result is \textbf{downstream readout reversal despite causal-role
recurrence}. On a prospectively matched population of 300 source pairs, the
selected-versus-control displacement has positive mean local task-readout
alignment at 370M and negative mean alignment at 1.4B
(\(t(299)=10.77\), one-sided \(p=2.22\times10^{-23}\)). A separate 130M
population supplies a positive contextual point, but we do not fit a scaling
law or infer a parameter-count threshold. Supporting behavioral and AVeriTeC
experiments delimit the downstream and external scope, while a fixed-mirror
steering test separates mechanistic validity from control utility.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Prior work has established detailed mechanistic analysis in Mamba through
causal tracing and editing \citep{sharma2024locating}, knockout-style
information-flow analysis \citep{endy2025knockout}, and Mamba-specific
relevance propagation \citep{jafari2024mambalrp}. Cross-model work compares
features and circuits across Transformers and Mambas
\citep{wang2025universality,arora2025mechanistic}; activation-subspace methods
also provide explicit SSM intervention objects \citep{mohan2026subspace}.
Non-unique causal localization in Mamba-2 further shows that representational
similarity and causal function need not coincide \citep{jiang2026circuit}, and
recent work studies mechanistic scaling in Mamba associative recall
\citep{koren2026recall}. Our contribution is narrower: conditioning on
recurrence of a causally validated role, we test two stronger implications---
fixed geometric realization and stable downstream task alignment. We make no
first-ever interpretability claim, universality claim, or model-size scaling
claim.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Checkpoint-local geometry and prospective populations.}
We analyze frozen pretrained Mamba checkpoints at 130M, 370M, and 1.4B
parameters. For each checkpoint, XG2/XG4 response-free rows define independent
local geometry and the structurally independent XG1 generator supplies
prospective response populations. Plane vectors, selected/control ranks, and
strong-channel identities are never transferred across checkpoints. The
primary finite-difference scale is \(\epsilon=0.025\). For a unit direction
\(w\), local susceptibility is
\(J_i(w)=[F_i(+\epsilon;w)-F_i(-\epsilon;w)]/(2\epsilon)\); the frozen XG2--XG4
contrast defines the checkpoint-local response endpoint \(Q\). No basis is
refit using XG1 responses. Where formal inference is performed, the unit is the
predeclared item or matched source-pair population for that study;
response-free geometry rows and stagewise summaries remain descriptive.
Cross-checkpoint tests use matched source pairs only where explicitly
specified. Exact construction details and cohort identities are in
Appendix~\ref{app:methods}.

\paragraph{130M validation and cross-checkpoint recurrence.}
At 130M, PP3 was frozen before prospective validation. Four disjoint XG1
populations test external transport, specificity against response-blind PP5
(the maximum projector-separation plane), matched-control necessity, and local
restoration against an equal-coefficient PP5 replacement. Smaller
\(\epsilon\) values are descriptive robustness checks. At larger checkpoints,
independent discovery chooses the scale-local dominant component and a
response-blind control before disjoint confirmation. The confirmation endpoint
is the restored selected component minus the matched control. Residual planes
are characterized descriptively; no cross-checkpoint residual p-value is
introduced.

\paragraph{Adjacent-site transport.}
At 1.4B, the canonical source/target/intervention triplet is \((33,34,35)\) and
one architecture-predeclared adjacent \(+1\) triplet is \((34,35,36)\). The
canonical P5 subspace is transported through the intervening block and compared
with the independently reconstructed adjacent P5 plane on response-free rows.
Functional relevance is tested on a frozen \(N=300\) XG1 population with
\(G=D_{\mathrm{TRANSPORT}}-D_{\mathrm{ADJ}}\). A separate mandatory gate
requires \(\mathrm{mean}(D_{\mathrm{TRANSPORT}})>0\); relative shift and
positive restoration are therefore distinct.

\paragraph{Downstream readout and behavior.}
The primary cross-checkpoint readout compares 370M and 1.4B on the same
\(N=300\) source pairs. For task margin
\(m=z_y-\max_{c\neq y}z_c\), a local gradient is taken at the unmodified
intervention state while the downstream computation remains intact. The frozen
selected and coefficient-matched response-blind control components define
\(\Delta L=g^\top(C_{\mathrm{sel}}-C_{\mathrm{ctrl}})\), averaged within
source pair. The matched endpoint is
\(R_q=\Delta L_{370\mathrm{M},q}-\Delta L_{1.4\mathrm{B},q}\). The historical
\texttt{G3-GROUP-D-HALF} graph applies an intentional one-half D-edge gradient
ownership factor while preserving the forward value, so
\(\Delta L_{\mathrm{forward}}=2\Delta L_{\mathrm{owned}}\). This positive
rescaling changes derivative units but not signs, rankings, t statistics, or
p-values. Direct behavior uses the same selected-versus-control displacement;
stagewise summaries are descriptive.

\paragraph{External transfer, steering, and statistics.}
AVeriTeC transfer uses the official development source restricted to the 462
compatible three-class examples and annotated gold evidence; no retrieval or
question generation is executed. Causal margin transfer is distinct from a
separate 370M fixed-mirror steering utility test. Prospective contrasts use
predeclared one-sided Student t tests and only predeclared multiplicity
families receive correction. Failed gates are not rescued by redefining
population, plane, layer, token, tail, or endpoint. The three checkpoints are
fixed models, not a random sample of independently trained seeds.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{A causal recurrent-state role survives prospective validation}
\label{sec:r31}

In Mamba-130M, static localization identified PP3 as the dominant shared
positive contrast component, while substantial response remained in other
planes. On a fresh XG1 population (\(N=300\)), PP3 transported with mean
\(7.12\times10^{-8}\) (\(t(299)=18.97\), \(p=1.52\times10^{-53}\)). On a
second holdout, PP3 exceeded response-blind max-separation PP5 by
\(D_{\mathrm{SPEC}}=2.30\times10^{-8}\)
(\(t(299)=7.66\), \(p=1.34\times10^{-13}\)). Matched-control necessity and
restoration were also positive:
\(D_{\mathrm{NEC}}=4.74\times10^{-8}\)
(\(t(299)=23.98\), \(p=6.22\times10^{-72}\)) and
\(D_{\mathrm{SUF}}=4.08\times10^{-8}\)
(\(t(299)=20.11\), \(p=9.01\times10^{-58}\)). Here ``restoration-sufficient''
means restoration of the local susceptibility contrast relative to matched
PP5, not global or downstream behavioral sufficiency. PP3 remained uniquely
dominant at \(\epsilon=0.025,0.0125,0.00625\) with normalized profile cosine
similarity above 0.99994 (descriptive only). Figure~\ref{fig:validation}
summarizes this prospective validation chain.

\subsection{The causal role recurs while residual geometry reorganizes}
\label{sec:r32}

At 370M, independent reconstruction selected P3 against P5; fresh confirmation
gave mean \(D_{\mathrm{DOM}}=3.97\times10^{-8}\),
\(t(299)=26.06\), Holm-adjusted \(p=6.39\times10^{-79}\), with 94\% positive
pairs. At 1.4B, independent reconstruction instead selected P5 against P4;
confirmation gave mean \(D_{\mathrm{CORE}}=9.28\times10^{-9}\),
\(t(299)=16.25\), \(p=2.62\times10^{-43}\), with 0.783 positive-pair fraction.
Thus the invariant is not plane rank but the \emph{scale-local dominant causal
role} under the same reconstruction and matched-control protocol.

The surrounding residual mechanism reorganizes (Figure~\ref{fig:recurrence}).
At 370M residual mass concentrates on local P5 (\(\approx0.695\)), whereas at
1.4B it concentrates on P2 (\(\approx0.574\)) and P3
(\(\approx0.318\)); signed residual contributions and generator-family
coupling change as well. Residual signed sums closely track aggregate residuals
within each larger checkpoint (\(r=0.963\) and \(r=0.995\)), but these
cross-checkpoint residual comparisons are descriptive. We therefore summarize
the tested pattern as \textbf{core-stable / residual-plastic}, not a fixed-rank
identity or scaling law.

\subsection{Cross-block transport is functionally relevant but incomplete}
\label{sec:r33}

At 1.4B, the canonical \((33,34,35)\) site has positive P5-versus-P4 mean
\(D_{\mathrm{CAN}}=9.10\times10^{-9}\), while the single prospectively fixed
adjacent \((34,35,36)\) site has negative
\(D_{\mathrm{ADJ}}=-1.55\times10^{-9}\). Their paired difference is
\(1.07\times10^{-8}\) (\(t(299)=18.46\),
\(p=1.34\times10^{-51}\)); this establishes specificity only relative to that
one adjacent site.

Across 600 response-free XG2/XG4 rows, the transported rank-two canonical P5
subspace is strongly reoriented relative to the independently reconstructed
adjacent P5 plane (principal angles \(86.45^\circ\) and \(89.22^\circ\); mean
projector overlap 0.00231). On the frozen \(N=300\) response population,
transport raises the adjacent response by
\(G=1.00\times10^{-9}\) (\(t(299)=18.58\),
\(p=4.38\times10^{-52}\)), yet the transported mean remains negative
(\(D_{\mathrm{TRANSPORT}}=-5.51\times10^{-10}\)). Thus the relative-shift test
passes while the mandatory positive-restoration gate fails
(Figure~\ref{fig:transport}). The geometric reorientation is therefore
functionally relevant under the frozen transport test and contributes causally
to the local functional difference without fully accounting for it; no
mediated/explained percentage is assigned.

\subsection{Downstream readout reverses in the matched 370M--1.4B comparison}
\label{sec:r34}

We next asked whether recurrence of the internal role preserves downstream
task alignment. On the same 300 source pairs, the frozen selected/control
geometry is P3/P5 at 370M and P5/P4 at 1.4B. Mean ownership-weighted local
readout is positive at 370M
(\(+5.09\times10^{-4}\)) and negative at 1.4B
(\(-1.20\times10^{-3}\)). The prospectively defined matched difference is
\(1.70\times10^{-3}\) (SD \(2.74\times10^{-3}\)), with
\(t(299)=10.77\) and one-sided \(p=2.22\times10^{-23}\); both sign gates pass.
Under the deterministic ownership correction, the forward-equivalent means are
\(+1.02\times10^{-3}\) and \(-2.39\times10^{-3}\), respectively, without
changing inference.

A separately frozen 130M population has positive mean readout, but it is a
contextual point rather than part of the matched inferential sample. We do not
fit a three-point scaling regression or infer across independently trained
model seeds. Within the tested fixed checkpoints, causal-role recurrence does
not guarantee stable downstream readout orientation
(Figure~\ref{fig:readout}).

\subsection{Behavioral and external consequences are checkpoint- and endpoint-specific}
\label{sec:r35}

Direct intervention on the same matched population gives a positive 370M
behavioral bridge, mean \(D_{\mathrm{BEH}}=+1.12\times10^{-3}\)
(\(t(299)=5.82\), Holm-adjusted \(p=1.50\times10^{-8}\)), but the 1.4B mean is
negative (\(-2.01\times10^{-3}\)); because the pre-specified family tests for a
positive bridge, the 1.4B positive-bridge criterion is not established. A
stagewise descriptive analysis shows the first persistent entitlement-sensitive
opposition at \texttt{post\_block\_35} and pair-level opposition only at
\texttt{post\_block\_47}; it does not identify a unique mediator.

AVeriTeC provides an external boundary (Table~\ref{tab:external-transfer}). On
462 compatible gold-evidence examples, positive causal margin transfer is
supported at 130M and 370M. The 1.4B point estimate is negative but its
one-sided \(p=0.0757\), so negative transfer is not established. A separate
370M fixed-mirror steering test over 2,799 examples produces zero corrections
and zero damages; useful steering is therefore not established under that
fixed rule. Mechanistic validity, external causal transfer, and prediction-level
control utility are distinct endpoints.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Mechanistic recurrence is not coordinate invariance.}
The experiments separate three properties that can otherwise be conflated:
recurrence of a causal role, preservation of its geometric realization, and
preservation of downstream task alignment. The first property is functional.
At each tested checkpoint, an independently reconstructed component occupies
the dominant causal role relative to a frozen response-blind control. The
coordinate-level realization is less stable: the selected rank changes from P3
at 370M to P5 at 1.4B, while residual mass, signed effects, and generator-family
coupling reorganize. The \textbf{core-stable / residual-plastic} summary is
therefore deliberately role-level. It does not posit a shared latent axis or a
same-numbered plane identity across checkpoints.

The cross-block transport experiment sharpens this distinction. The canonical
1.4B P5 geometry becomes nearly orthogonal to the independently reconstructed
adjacent P5 plane, and transporting the canonical subspace produces a large
paired upward shift relative to the native adjacent response. This shows that
the geometric reorientation is functionally relevant under the frozen
transport test. Yet the transported mean remains negative and fails the
positive-restoration gate. Geometry therefore explains part of the local
functional change without exhausting it. We intentionally leave the remaining
discrepancy unresolved rather than converting ratios of canonical, adjacent,
and transported means into a post-hoc mediation percentage.

\paragraph{Downstream alignment is a second independent property.}
The matched 370M--1.4B readout result supplies a different separation. Even
when a dominant internal role recurs, its selected-versus-control displacement
can couple to the downstream correct-class margin with opposite mean local
orientation. The factor-of-two ownership correction changes derivative units,
not this interpretation: it is a deterministic positive rescaling induced by
the frozen surrogate-gradient graph. The substantive observation is the sign
reversal on the same 300 source pairs. Direct behavioral intervention is
consistent with the same bounded picture: positive downstream coupling is
established at 370M, whereas the prospectively specified positive bridge is not
established at 1.4B. The stagewise analysis localizes where this divergence
becomes readable but does not identify a unique mediator.

Together these results suggest a layered notion of mechanistic recurrence.
One can recover an analogous causal role while the local coordinates realizing
that role and the downstream orientation through which it affects a task have
changed. In that setting, ``the mechanism recurs'' is not enough to justify
copying an intervention vector, assuming a stable readout sign, or interpreting
a same-ranked subspace as semantically identical.

\paragraph{Implications for mechanistic transfer and control.}
The operational consequence is revalidation rather than a claim of general
non-transferability. If a mechanism is transferred to another checkpoint or
scale, at least two questions should be reopened: whether the local geometry
still realizes the same role, and whether perturbing that geometry still
moves the downstream task objective in the intended direction. The present
study shows that those questions can have different answers even when the
role-level causal test succeeds.

The external experiments provide a related boundary. Positive causal margin
transfer on AVeriTeC gold-evidence inputs at 130M and 370M shows that the
synthetic causal object is not confined to the XG1 generator. However, this
margin-level transfer does not imply useful control. The fixed-mirror steering
rule at 370M changes no argmax predictions despite the validated internal
mechanism and external margin effect. Conversely, the null steering result does
not invalidate the mechanism: it only shows that one fixed transformation did
not convert that mechanism into prediction-level utility. Mechanistic validity,
external causal transfer, and controllability are therefore separate empirical
questions.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The scope is intentionally bounded. We study three fixed pretrained Mamba
checkpoints, not a population of independently trained seeds, so the
matched-item inference does not define a model-population scaling law, phase
transition, or parameter threshold. All primary evidence is within the tested
Mamba family and does not establish architecture-independent universality.
The geometry is procedure-defined and checkpoint-local; residual
cross-checkpoint comparisons remain descriptive rather than inferential.
Cross-block transport is incomplete, and the site-specificity experiment uses
one prospectively fixed adjacent site rather than a layer sweep. The
susceptibility analysis operates at finite \(\epsilon\), with smaller values
used only for descriptive robustness. AVeriTeC uses the compatible three-class
gold-evidence subset without retrieval, and the 1.4B negative point estimate
does not pass its pre-specified alpha gate. Finally, steering evaluates one
preregistered fixed-mirror rule and cannot support a general claim of
unsteerability. Expanded methodological and scope details appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:methods}--\ref{app:limitations}.
