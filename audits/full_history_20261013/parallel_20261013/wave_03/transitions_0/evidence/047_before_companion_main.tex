\title{Causal-Role Recurrence, Geometric Variation, and Objective-Conditioned Readout Across Mamba-1 Scales}
\author{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}

Mechanistic comparisons across model scales can conflate three questions: whether independently reconstructed components play an operationally matched \textbf{causal role}, whether their native \textbf{geometry} is preserved, and how an \textbf{objective} reads them. We separate these questions in pretrained Mamba-1 models from 130M to 2.8B parameters.

At 130M, prospective transport, response-independent specificity, matched necessity, and restoration establish a recurrent-state mechanism on disjoint populations. At larger scales, a frozen discover-then-confirm protocol selects one of five local planes on a discovery cohort, fixes a response-blind geometric control, and evaluates the pair on a disjoint confirmation cohort. The selected-restored versus matched-control contrast is positive under each scale's frozen confirmation, while a distinct historical 370M residual criterion remains failed.

Coordinate-insensitive comparison of matched response-blind XG2/XG4 populations yields heterogeneous, intermediate cross-scale similarity (centered linear CKA: XG2 $0.34$--$0.74$; XG4 $0.39$--$0.59$), with the same qualitative pattern under cosine-RSM. For the primary frozen native contrasts, downstream task and pretrained next-token objectives give opposed five-scale point-estimate sign vectors. Pair-resampling intervals exclude zero in 8 of 10 scale-by-objective cells, and a 1.4B sensitivity contrast changes the pretrained-LM sign, showing that the aggregate orientation is contrast-conditioned.

Thus a scale-local interventional role can recur without shared plane identity or exact geometric invariance, while functional readout depends on both objective and the frozen native contrast. We frame this as a controlled Mamba-1 case study rather than a universal scaling law.

\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

When mechanistic structure is compared across model sizes, ``the same mechanism'' can refer to different objects. We distinguish \textbf{causal role} (what changes under a matched intervention), \textbf{geometric realization} (how that role is represented in native state space), and \textbf{objective-conditioned functional meaning} (how a particular objective reads the state). Throughout, \emph{recurrence} means recurrence of an operationally matched causal role: independently reconstructed scale-local components satisfy the same selected-restored versus matched-control intervention contrast on fresh confirmation data. It does not mean shared coordinates, principal-plane identity, or semantic homology across models.

We study five pretrained Mamba-1 models: 130M, 370M, 790M, 1.4B, and 2.8B. We first establish one recurrent-state mechanism deeply at 130M using prospective transport, response-independent geometric specificity, matched necessity, and restoration. Larger scales then use a frozen discover-then-confirm protocol rather than reusing the 130M plane identity.

The first cross-scale result is a separation. A scale-local causal role survives its own frozen matched-control confirmation at every tested size even though the selected principal-plane rank changes. In contrast, response-blind alignment geometry shows heterogeneous, intermediate cross-scale conservation: centered linear CKA spans $0.34$--$0.74$ for XG2 and $0.39$--$0.59$ for XG4, with secondary cosine-RSM giving the same qualitative picture. We therefore find that \emph{exact CKA-level invariance does not hold on these frozen cohorts}, without claiming that cross-scale similarity is absent or unexpectedly low under a calibrated null.

The second result concerns functional use. For each scale's primary frozen native contrast, the downstream task and pretrained next-token objectives yield opposed five-scale point-estimate sign vectors. Eight of ten scale-by-objective cells are individually sign-stable under pair resampling; the two smallest-magnitude cells are not. A sensitivity contrast at 1.4B also changes the pretrained-LM sign, so the objective result is explicitly contrast-conditioned rather than a universal scale-level orientation.

These findings motivate three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Operational causal-role recurrence.} Independently reconstructed scale-local components survive a common selected-restored versus matched-control criterion on fresh confirmations; the recurring object is procedural and interventional, not a fixed plane.
\item \textbf{Geometric variation with partial conservation.} Response-blind CKA and cosine-RSM show stable, heterogeneous intermediate cross-scale similarity rather than exact invariance.
\item \textbf{Contrast-conditioned objective readout.} Fixed primary native contrasts exhibit different point-estimate aggregate orientations under downstream and pretrained objectives, with uncertainty concentrated in the smallest-magnitude cells and explicit sensitivity to contrast choice.
\end{enumerate}

Behavioral and external-transfer experiments bound how far these mechanistic findings extend beyond the local state.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Mechanistic interpretability of Mamba and state-space models.}

Mamba replaces attention with input-dependent selective state-space dynamics, making recurrent state a natural object for mechanistic analysis \citep{gu2024mamba}. Ensign and Garriga-Alonso partially reverse-engineer the indirect-object-identification circuit in Mamba, identifying a key bottleneck, convolution-mediated token shifting, and linear storage of name information in the SSM state \citep{ensign2024ioi}. Wang et al. compare Transformers and Mambas using sparse autoencoders and circuit analysis, finding substantial feature similarity and structurally analogous induction mechanisms while also identifying Mamba-specific differences \citep{wang2025universality}. More recent work identifies activation-subspace bottlenecks across several state-space models and tests their causal relevance through interventions and steering \citep{mohan2026subspace}.

Our question is complementary. Rather than asking whether Mamba contains an interpretable feature or circuit for a particular benchmark, or whether two architectures implement analogous algorithms, we ask whether a \textbf{causal role identified inside one Mamba scale recurs after native geometry is independently reconstructed at larger scales}. We then separate that recurrence from both geometric invariance and downstream functional interpretation.

\paragraph{Representation similarity and cross-model recurrence.}

Representational-similarity methods such as centered kernel alignment provide coordinate-insensitive tools for comparing neural representations \citep{kornblith2019cka}. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis further argues that representations may become increasingly aligned across models and modalities as capability grows \citep{huh2024platonic}. At the same time, recent work shows that representational-similarity conclusions can depend strongly on objectives and evaluation datasets \citep{ciernik2025objective}, and formal work on linear representations emphasizes that geometric interpretation itself depends on the structure used to compare representational spaces \citep{park2024linear}. Sun and Toneva formalize \emph{interpretive equivalence} across neural networks and derive conditions relating representations, circuits, and algorithmic interpretations \citep{sun2026equivalent}.

Our empirical question is narrower and complementary. We do not require two models to share a direction, a principal-plane rank, a globally aligned coordinate system, or an interpretation in the formal equivalence sense. We ask whether independently reconstructed components can satisfy the same \textbf{operational interventional role}, and then test whether response-blind geometry and objective-conditioned readout recur with it.

\paragraph{Causal interventions and functional interpretation.}

Mechanistic interpretability increasingly distinguishes correlational localization from causal evidence. Prior work has used interventions and causal tracing to localize model computations, edit factual associations, and formalize mechanistic claims using causal abstractions \citep{dai2022knowledge,meng2022rome,geiger2023causal}. Our protocol follows the same general principle that localization alone is insufficient, but places additional emphasis on prospective matched controls. At 130M, a localized component must transport to fresh data, outperform a response-independent geometric control, show selective necessity, and recover the response under matched restoration before serving as the basis for cross-scale comparison.

Finally, representation and causal role do not uniquely determine how a state is used by a particular objective. Our setting permits a controlled comparison: within each Mamba scale, the native contrast and source population are held fixed while its local functional readout is evaluated under either the structured downstream task objective or the original pretrained next-token objective. We therefore distinguish \textbf{objective-conditioned functional meaning} from both native geometry and causal-role recurrence.

All three controlled families use the same six-cell title/name/role/predicate slot-substitution design over synthetic structured records. XG1 is an independently generated record family reserved for causal-response and behavioral holdouts. XG2 and XG4 are disjoint response-blind generator families with different controlled record templates and lexicons (bulletin-style versus register-style) used to reconstruct native geometry. Thus $Q=E_{\rm XG2}-E_{\rm XG4}$ denotes relative squared local susceptibility to these two controlled generator families; it is not claimed to encode a natural semantic dichotomy. The XG2/XG4 contrast is intentionally controlled rather than semantically natural: it provides a response-independent comparison axis on which recurrent-state susceptibility can be localized and prospectively tested. Its scientific role is as a reproducible mechanistic probe, not as a claim that the two generator families instantiate a latent semantic variable.


\subsection{Deep causal establishment at Mamba-130M}
\label{sec:deep}

We first establish the local recurrent-state mechanism in Mamba-130M before asking whether any part of it recurs at larger scales. Here, a \textbf{causal role} means that manipulating a scale-local native-state component changes a pre-specified recurrent-state endpoint relative to matched controls, and that restoring the component recovers that endpoint.

Starting from controlled semantic contrasts, we localized a dominant native-state component, PP3, and evaluated it through prospective experiments on disjoint XG1 controlled-generator holdouts (Fig.~\ref{fig:deep}A). PP3 denotes the third principal plane of the independently reconstructed 130M geometry; its rank label is local to this model and does not denote a globally shared semantic feature.

The localized PP3 effect first transported to an independent 300-pair population. A second holdout then compared PP3 against PP5, selected independently from response values as a geometric control, and showed that PP3 retained the stronger effect. Subsequent matched interventions established both necessity and restoration sufficiency: neutralizing PP3 attenuated the endpoint more strongly than the control intervention, while restoring the exact PP3 component recovered the endpoint more strongly than an equal-norm PP5 replacement. All four pre-specified tests supported their respective hypotheses. Exact test statistics are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

The 130M intervention also reached the downstream task computation. Restoring PP3 instead of the matched PP5 replacement increased the downstream task margin by $0.00833$ on average over 300 paired items (Fig.~\ref{fig:deep}B). This was a small continuous margin effect rather than an accuracy gain; discrete prediction changes were negligible. The $10^{-8}$-scale $Q$ means reported for the local causal tests are in the internal endpoint's native units and should not be read as downstream behavioral-effect magnitudes; their very small $p$-values reflect consistency relative to paired variation. Across source pairs, the local task-functional readout tracked the behavioral restoration effect with Pearson $r=0.618$, Spearman $\rho=0.832$, and 82.3\% sign agreement (Fig.~\ref{fig:deep}C). These associations are descriptive and were not used to select the mechanism.

The object carried into the scale study is therefore not ``PP3'' as a fixed semantic plane. It is an operationally matched, scale-local recurrent-state causal role under a frozen scientific procedure.

\subsection{Operational causal-role recurrence with partial geometric conservation}
\label{sec:recurrence}

We next ask two questions: \emph{does an operationally matched causal role survive at larger scales, and how much of the response-blind geometry is conserved?}

The selected/control pairs are P3/P5 at 130M, P3/P5 at 370M, P2/P5 at 790M, P5/P4 at 1.4B, and P3/P5 at 2.8B (Fig.~\ref{fig:scale}A). These are scale-local identities, not same-numbered-plane homologies. At 130M, P3 was localized from response-blind XG2/XG4 geometry before XG1 outcomes. At later scales, all five reconstructed planes were evaluated only on a dedicated discovery cohort; the unique mean-response argmax was frozen, a non-selected control was chosen from frozen geometry alone, and the pair was then evaluated on a disjoint confirmation cohort. The discovery winner is therefore a selection rule rather than confirmatory evidence, and the primary claim is matched-control confirmation rather than omnibus superiority over every other plane.

Under the common algebraic contrast $Q_{\mathrm{selected\ restored}}-Q_{\mathrm{matched\ control}}$, each scale's frozen confirmation is positive. At 370M, however, a distinct historical positive aggregate-residual criterion failed; later core confirmations do not retroactively alter that failed criterion (Appendix~\ref{app:scale}). Raw $Q$ magnitudes are endpoint-specific internal units and are not treated as a scaling series.

For each scale and response-blind family (XG2 and XG4), the frozen geometry preparation provides 300 \texttt{alignment\_delta\_h} vectors on the same ordered source-pair population. Row-normalized vectors define sample Gram matrices, which we compare across scales using centered linear CKA; cosine-RSM is secondary. The off-diagonal CKA values are heterogeneous and intermediate rather than near either identity or zero: XG2 spans $0.34$--$0.74$ and XG4 $0.39$--$0.59$. Joint source-pair resampling preserves this qualitative structure, while split halves show stronger fine-grained ordering stability for XG2 than XG4 (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

These values establish lack of \emph{exact} invariance under the stated CKA comparison while retaining nonzero cross-scale conservation. We do not estimate a within-model noise ceiling or a cross-model null distribution, and therefore do not claim that the observed similarities are unexpectedly low relative to such a baseline. The analysis also does not establish non-equivalence under arbitrary invertible transformations or characterize the complete hidden-state representation.

\subsection{Task-functional coupling reorganizes across scale}
\label{sec:coupling}

Even though the causal role recurs, the downstream task does not use the selected component in the same aggregate way at every scale. The mean forward-equivalent readout is positive at 130M, 370M, and 790M and negative at 1.4B and 2.8B:
\[
(+0.00224,\;+0.00102,\;+0.00455,\;-0.00239,\;-0.00015).
\]
We treat these point-estimate aggregate signs $+,+,+,-,-$ as a descriptive five-scale pattern, not as a parameter threshold or monotonic scaling law.

For pair $i$, the local readout is
\[
\Delta L_i
=
g_i^\top
\left(
c_{\mathrm{sel},i}-c_{\mathrm{ctrl},i}
\right),
\]
where $g_i$ is the gradient of the downstream task margin with respect to the frozen local recurrent-state value. This makes $\Delta L$ explicitly objective-conditioned rather than an intrinsic property of the pretrained state.

The exact decomposition in Appendix~\ref{app:decomp} separates gradient magnitude, component magnitude, angular alignment, and their dependence terms. It shows that no single geometric scalar explains the aggregate signs: at 370M dependence terms overcome a slightly negative mean alignment gap; at 1.4B negative task-gradient alignment dominates; and at 2.8B adverse dependence outweighs a positive mean gap. The same recurring native causal role can therefore couple differently to the downstream objective across scale.

\subsection{Functional readout is objective- and contrast-conditioned}
\label{sec:objective}

The task-objective pattern could still reflect a functional organization already present under Mamba's pretrained objective. We test that alternative by keeping each scale's source population, target site, selected component, and matched control fixed while replacing the downstream task margin with the pretrained next-token log-probability objective.

For the primary task-matched native contrasts, point-estimate aggregate signs are $+,+,+,-,-$ under the task objective and $-,-,-,+,+$ under the pretrained next-token objective (Fig.~\ref{fig:objective}). Pair-resampling intervals exclude zero in 8 of 10 scale-by-objective cells; the 370M pretrained-LM and 2.8B task readouts cross zero and therefore lack individual sign stability under this analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}). The prospective LM-control family covered the four larger scales; the later 130M completeness extension is not retroactively pooled into it (Appendix~\ref{app:vanilla}).

The opposed point-estimate means do not imply rowwise inversion: pair-level Pearson and Spearman associations vary in sign, and sign agreement remains near the middle of its range. More importantly, the aggregate result is \emph{contrast-conditioned}. In the common-P3/P5 sensitivity comparison, the 1.4B pretrained-LM mean is negative whereas its primary task-matched P5/P4 pretrained-LM mean is positive (Appendix~\ref{app:vanilla}). We therefore interpret the evidence as objective dependence of each frozen native contrast, not as a universal scale-level orientation or a contrast-invariant sign reversal.

\subsection{Functional consequences extend beyond the local readout, but remain bounded}
\label{sec:scope}

We finally ask how far the local mechanism propagates beyond the controlled recurrent-state analysis. These experiments serve as scope tests rather than a third headline contribution.

At 130M, the mechanism reaches the learned task computation: restoring PP3 relative to the matched PP5 replacement shifts the downstream task margin and tracks the local functional readout across pairs, as established in Sec.~\ref{sec:deep}. The effect is primarily margin-level rather than a discrete prediction improvement.

We additionally evaluate external transfer on the official AVeriTeC development set \citep{schlichtkrull2023averitec} using a common 462-example three-label gold-evidence cohort. This experiment is available at 130M, 370M, and 1.4B only. The selected-versus-control intervention produces small positive correct-class margin shifts at 130M ($2.31\times10^{-4}$, $d_z=0.120$) and 370M ($4.56\times10^{-5}$, $d_z=0.106$); both positive-transfer hypotheses are supported under their frozen prospective family.

At 1.4B, the point estimate changes sign to $-7.85\times10^{-5}$, but the pre-specified one-sided negative test does not pass the alpha gate ($p=0.0757$). We therefore do not claim an established external sign reversal. The 1.4B intervention also changes no argmax predictions among the 462 examples, and all intervention conditions retain the same accuracy.

Because the frozen downstream model was not trained on AVeriTeC and this is not a benchmark-performance study, we use the experiment only as an out-of-distribution natural-language margin probe. The external evidence therefore supports a bounded conclusion: the native-state intervention can propagate to continuous task and natural-language decision margins, but the available evidence does not establish uniform behavioral improvement, scale-invariant external transfer, or benchmark-level correction.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Design, sites, and native geometry.}
We analyze fixed pretrained Mamba-1 backbones from 130M to 2.8B. Principal-plane ranks are model-local. The 130M causal study froze intervention layer 17 of 24. Larger-scale target sites were not re-localized from XG1 outcomes: the intervention layer is mapped by relative depth,
$\ell_s=\mathrm{round}[(17/(24-1))(L_s-1)]$,
giving layer 35 for the 48-layer 370M/790M/1.4B models and layer 47 for the 64-layer 2.8B model, while preserving source/target-residual offsets $(-2,-1)$. Geometry preparation uses native Mamba activations without XG1 outcomes, downstream task logits or gradients, or backward passes.

Each scale supplies frozen 300-row XG2/XG4 \texttt{alignment\_delta\_h} matrices. Row-normalized vectors give sample Grams $K_s=U_sU_s^\top$; normalized Frobenius products of centered Grams give linear CKA, while Pearson correlation of the strict upper cosine-Gram triangles gives secondary cosine-RSM. These frozen static values are transcribed without figure-builder recomputation; provenance and scalar measurements are in Appendix~\ref{app:geometry}.

\paragraph{Plane selection and causal confirmation.}
At 130M, P3 was selected from response-blind XG2/XG4 geometry before XG1 response inspection, and P5 was frozen independently as the maximum-projector-separation geometric control. For the later dominant-component protocol, all five reconstructed planes are evaluated on a dedicated discovery population and the selected rank is frozen as
$k_s^*=\operatorname{unique\,argmax}_k\,\overline{[Q_{\mathrm{restored}}(k)-Q_{\mathrm{neutralized}}(k)]}$.
No positivity gate or inferential $p$-value is used in discovery. The control is selected separately from frozen geometry among non-selected planes,
$c_s^*=\arg\max_{k\neq k_s^*}\lambda_{s,k}^{+}$.
The selected/control pair is then frozen before a disjoint confirmation population is opened, where the pre-specified selected-restored versus matched-control endpoint is tested once. Confirmation responses cannot reselect the plane, control, tail, layer, offset, or epsilon. This design prevents same-sample argmax confirmation, while making no omnibus claim that the selected plane is superior to every alternative plane. Historical endpoints and the failed 370M residual criterion are retained rather than harmonized away (Appendix~\ref{app:causal}--\ref{app:scale}).

\paragraph{Task-functional readout.}
The separately trained structured downstream model supplies a correct-class task margin. At each frozen native state, its local gradient defines
$\Delta L_i=g_i^\top(c_{\mathrm{sel},i}-c_{\mathrm{ctrl},i})$.
Selected and control components have numerically equal norms. The frozen branch-ownership convention gives
$\Delta L_{\rm forward}=2\Delta L_{\rm owned}$, preserving signs and correlations. The exact algebraic decomposition is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:decomp}.

\paragraph{Pretrained-objective control.}
Within each scale, the native contrast, source population, and target site remain fixed while the objective becomes the original next-token log probability. The original prospective control covers 370M--2.8B; the already-studied 130M cell was completed later under a separate frozen extension. The five-scale display and pair-level associations are descriptive (Appendix~\ref{app:vanilla}).

\paragraph{Behavior, external transfer, and inference.}
At 130M, restoration and control are compared on a continuous task margin. External transfer uses the fixed three-label AVeriTeC subset ($N=462$) at 130M, 370M, and 1.4B. The 130M/370M positive tests form one frozen family; the later 1.4B negative test is separate. We neither pool them nor estimate a cross-scale zero crossing (Appendix~\ref{app:external} gives serialization, tokenizer, and inference details).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The strongest conclusion is procedural: across five Mamba-1 checkpoints, a scale-local component selected under a frozen protocol survives a pre-specified matched-control confirmation, despite changing principal-plane rank. This is recurrence of an operational interventional role, not evidence that the models contain one coordinate-identical or semantically homologous ``same mechanism.'' The 370M aggregate-residual criterion remains a genuine historical failure and is not rescued by later confirmations.

Response-blind alignment geometry is neither identical nor absent across scales. Centered linear CKA and cosine-RSM show heterogeneous intermediate conservation, and source-pair resampling preserves that structure. Because we do not estimate a noise ceiling or cross-model null, the geometric claim is limited to lack of exact invariance under the stated comparison; it does not quantify how surprising the observed CKA range should be for independently reconstructed models of different sizes.

Functional readout is likewise conditional. The primary frozen contrasts have opposed task-versus-pretrained point-estimate sign vectors, with pair-resampling support in 8 of 10 scale-by-objective cells, but the 1.4B pretrained-LM sign changes under the common-P3/P5 sensitivity contrast. We therefore use \emph{objective-conditioned functionalization} only to mean that a fixed native contrast can be read differently by different objectives; we do not claim a contrast-invariant or universal scale-level orientation.

The intervention reaches a continuous task margin at 130M and small external AVeriTeC margins at 130M/370M, while the 1.4B external negative point estimate misses its prospective gate. These scope tests do not establish benchmark improvement. More broadly, five checkpoints from one Mamba-1 family and one controlled mechanism constitute a carefully staged case study, not an architecture-general scaling law. Comparison with Mamba-2 or Transformer families remains future work.

Within these bounds, the study provides an empirical separation among three notions that are often conflated in cross-model mechanistic comparison: recurrence of an operational causal role, conservation of response-blind geometry, and objective-conditioned use of a frozen native contrast.
