ABSTRACT
Mechanistic comparisons across model scales often ask whether the same feature
or circuit reappears in larger models. This framing conflates at least three ques-
tions: whether an internal component plays the same causal role, whether that
role is realized by the same geometry, and whether the component has the same
functional meaning under different objectives. We separate these questions in pre-
trained Mamba-1 models from 130M to 2.8B parameters.
We first establish a recurrent-state mechanism deeply at 130M through prospec-
tive transport, response-independent geometric controls, matched necessity, and
restoration-sufficiency interventions. The five scale-local causal tests share a
selected-restored minus coefficient-matched-control contrast. Response-blind na-
tive kernel and leading-plane geometry is available at all five sizes: 130M comes
from an earlier reconstruction, while 370M–2.8B form a later matched bridge.
We next examine how these native components are used by a separately trained
structured downstream task model. Their task-functional alignment changes qual-
itatively across scale and cannot be explained by a single geometric statistic or
monotonic scaling trend. Finally, we hold each scale-specific native contrast
fixed and replace the downstream objective with Mamba’s original next-token
language-model objective. The resulting aggregate functional orientation differs
systematically from the task-objective readout across the complete five-scale de-
scriptive set, while pair-level behavior rules out a simple sign inversion.
Together, these results show thatcausal-role recurrence does not imply geomet-
ric invariance, and neither implies objective-independent functional mean-
ing. Mechanistic comparisons across scale should therefore distinguish what a
representation causally does, how a model realizes that function, and how a par-
ticular objective uses it.
1 INTRODUCTION
When a feature or circuit appears across model sizes, “the same mechanism” can mean different
things. We separatecausal role(the effect of a locally identified component under matched inter-
vention),geometric realization(its representation in native state space), andobjective-conditioned
functional meaning(how a particular objective reads it). A recurring intervention effect does not
require fixed coordinates, and geometry alone does not determine downstream use.
We study five pretrained Mamba-1 models: 130M, 370M, 790M, 1.4B, and 2.8B. Their recurrent
states permit local intervention while preserving the surrounding computation. We reconstruct na-
tive geometry independently within each scale; equal plane ranks across models carry no assumed
semantic correspondence. At 130M, prospective transport, a response-independent geometric con-
trol, matched necessity, and restoration sufficiency establish the causal mechanism on independent
populations. That chain grounds the later cross-scale comparison.
A scale-local causal role recurs at all five sizes, although selected plane rank varies. For the two plot-
ted native quantities, historical response-blind 130M measurements complement the later matched
370M–2.8B geometry bridge, which reorganizes across those larger checkpoints. To examine func-
tional use, we read each component under a structured downstream evidence-entitlement objective.

Our question is complementary. Rather than asking whether Mamba contains an interpretable fea-
ture or circuit for a particular benchmark, or whether two architectures implement analogous al-
gorithms, we ask whether acausal role identified inside one Mamba scale recurs after native
geometry is independently reconstructed at larger scales. We then separate that recurrence from
both geometric invariance and downstream functional interpretation.
Representation similarity and cross-model recurrence.Representational-similarity methods
such as centered kernel alignment provide coordinate-insensitive tools for comparing neural rep-
resentations (Kornblith et al., 2019). The Platonic Representation Hypothesis further argues that
representations may become increasingly aligned across models and modalities as capability grows
(Huh et al., 2024). At the same time, recent work shows that representational-similarity conclusions
can depend strongly on objectives and evaluation datasets (Ciernik et al., 2025), and formal work on
linear representations emphasizes that geometric interpretation itself depends on the structure used
to compare representational spaces (Park et al., 2024).
These results motivate a stricter notion of recurrence for mechanistic analysis. We do not require two
models to share a direction, a principal-plane rank, or a globally aligned coordinate system. Instead,
we ask whether independently reconstructed components satisfy comparableinterventional roles.
Causal interventions and functional interpretation.Mechanistic interpretability increasingly
distinguishes correlational localization from causal evidence. Prior work has used interventions and
causal tracing to localize model computations, edit factual associations, and formalize mechanistic
claims using causal abstractions (Dai et al., 2022; Meng et al., 2022; Geiger et al., 2023). Our pro-
tocol follows the same general principle that localization alone is insufficient, but places additional
emphasis on prospective matched controls. At 130M, a localized component must transport to fresh
data, outperform a response-independent geometric control, show selective necessity, and recover
the response under matched restoration before serving as the basis for cross-scale comparison.
Finally, representation and causal role do not uniquely determine how a state is used by a particular
objective. Our setting permits a controlled comparison: within each Mamba scale, the native contrast
and source population are held fixed while its local functional readout is evaluated under either the
structured downstream task objective or the original pretrained next-token objective. We therefore
distinguishobjective-conditioned functional meaningfrom both native geometry and causal-role
recurrence.

then compared PP3 against PP5, selected independently from response values as a geometric control,
and showed that PP3 retained the stronger effect. Subsequent matched interventions established

3.4 FUNCTIONAL ORIENTATION IS OBJECTIVE-CONDITIONED
The previous result leaves a direct alternative explanation: the task-objective pattern might simply
expose a functional organization already present under the pretrained language-model objective. We
test this by removing the downstream task model from the measurement and asking how the same
scale-specific native contrast is read by the original next-token objective.
Within each scale, we keep the frozen source population, target position, selected component, and
matched control fixed. Instead of differentiating a downstream task margin, we differentiate the
pretrained model’s teacher-forced log probability of the observed next token with respect to the
same local recurrent-state leaf. No downstream task checkpoint, router, class logit, or separately
trained task head enters this measurement.
The broader study already covered five Mamba-1 scales. The initial frozen vanilla-LM control
instantiated only 370M, 790M, 1.4B, and 2.8B, leaving the studied 130M scale uncovered. Across
those four, the task objective produced aggregate signs+,+,−,−, whereas the pretrained next-
token objective produced−,−,+,+. We subsequently completed the missing 130M cell under a
separately frozen extension using its pre-existing P3/P5 geometry and the same local next-token
objective. The complete five-scale descriptive vectors are+,+,+,−,−for the task objective and
−,−,−,+,+for vanilla LM.
The original prospective LM-control family remains the four larger scales; the later 130M result is
reported descriptively alongside it and is not retroactively pooled into that family.
The aggregate opposition is not a simple row-wise inversion. Across scales, Pearson and Spearman
associations between the two pair-level readouts vary in sign, and pairwise sign agreement remains
near the middle of its range rather than approaching zero. At 130M, for example, the pair-level
correlation is positive even though the aggregate means have opposite signs.
Nor does the result require cross-scale plane identity. Within each scale, the same frozen se-
lected/control contrast is supplied to both objectives; across scales, the plane ranks remain local.

3.5 FUNCTIONAL CONSEQUENCES EXTEND BEYOND THE LOCAL READOUT,BUT REMAIN
BOUNDED
We finally ask how far the local mechanism propagates beyond the controlled recurrent-state analy-
sis. These experiments serve as scope tests rather than a third headline contribution.
At 130M, the mechanism reaches the learned task computation: restoring PP3 relative to the matched
PP5 replacement shifts the downstream task margin and tracks the local functional readout across
pairs, as established in Sec. 3.1. The effect is primarily margin-level rather than a discrete prediction
improvement.
We additionally evaluate external transfer on the official A VeriTeC development set (Schlichtkrull
et al., 2023) using a common 462-example three-label gold-evidence cohort. This experiment is
available at 130M, 370M, and 1.4B only. The selected-versus-control intervention produces small
positive correct-class margin shifts at 130M (2.31×10 −4,d z = 0.120) and 370M (4.56×10 −5,
dz = 0.106); both positive-transfer hypotheses are supported under their frozen prospective family.

4 METHODS
Design and native geometry.We analyze fixed pretrained Mamba-1 backbones at 130M, 370M,
790M, 1.4B, and 2.8B parameters. Native-state principal planes and selected/control components
are reconstructed independently within each scale. Plane numbers identify components only within
a model. The two plotted response-blind geometry quantities have historical 130M values and a later
homogeneous 370M–2.8B bridge; these were not one five-scale execution. Geometry and control
selection use native Mamba activations without downstream task responses, logits, or gradients
from the held-out intervention population. Interventions act at frozen recurrent-state sites and token
anchors. Exact revisions, sites, masks, and cohorts appear in Appendix A.1–A.2.
Causal establishment and recurrence.The 130M component was established on successive dis-
joint 300-pair XG1 controlled-generator holdouts: transport of a localized effect, specificity against
a response-independent geometric control, matched necessity, and restoration sufficiency. Restora-
tion adds the exact removed selected component; the control adds a norm-matched component us-
ing the same native coefficients. Each test had a frozen direction before its response population
was inspected. Later scales use independently reconstructed geometry, a discovery population,
and a fresh confirmation population. All five causal tests useQ=E XG2 −E XG4, the differ-
ence in mean squared finite-difference susceptibility over five directions per family, and the contrast
Qselected restored −Qmatched control. The five experiments share this scale-local causal contrast while
retaining distinct historical confirmation protocols. The 130M algebra and the 370M two-hypothesis
Holm family appear in Appendix A.3–A.4. No cross-scale inferential family or magnitude scaling
is asserted.

5 DISCUSSION
The results separate causal role, geometric realization, and objective-conditioned functional mean-
ing. The same scale-local selected-restored versus matched-control causal contrast is supported at
five Mamba-1 sizes, under distinct historical confirmation protocols. Selected principal-plane rank
changes, and the two plotted response-blind native quantities include earlier 130M evidence along-
side the later 370M–2.8B bridge. Deep prospective transport, specificity, necessity, and restoration
tests at 130M ground this comparison: recurrence describes an interventional role reconstructed
within each model, not a universal same-numbered plane.
The task-functional readout changes sign across the sampled checkpoints, with distinct contributions
from gradient magnitude, component magnitude, and alignment. The+,+,+,−,−pattern does not
define a parameter threshold or an intrinsic representational reversal. Reading the same native con-
trasts under the original next-token objective instead yields opposite aggregate orientations from the
task-objective readout across the five-scale descriptive set. Pair-level correlations are not uniformly
negative, so aggregate opposition is not a rowwise inversion. We call thisobjective-conditioned
functionalization: a native recurrent substrate permits different functional organization under dif-
ferent objectives. The structured downstream task model provides the objective for this analysis,
without an architectural or benchmark-superiority claim.
Restoration at 130M reaches a continuous task margin, and positive external margin transfer is
supported at 130M and 370M. The 1.4B external point estimate is negative but misses its prospective
inferential gate. These outcomes show propagation beyond the local readout without establishing
uniform prediction improvement or scale-invariant external transfer.
The five fixed checkpoints do not identify a continuous scaling law. The two plotted native geom-
etry quantities have five-scale evidence, but 130M has earlier provenance than the homogeneous
four-scale bridge. The original pretrained-LM control also covered those four; its 130M cell was
completed later under a separately frozen protocol and joined only descriptively. External trans-
fer covers three sizes. Conclusions are local to the specified recurrent-state sites, token anchors,
populations, and Mamba-1 family. Within those bounds,causal-role recurrence does not imply
geometric invariance, and neither implies objective-independent functional meaning. Cross-
scale comparisons should test causal effect, native realization, and objective-specific use separately.
