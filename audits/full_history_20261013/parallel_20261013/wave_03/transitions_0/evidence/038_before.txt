ABSTRACT
Mechanistic comparisons across model scales can conflate three questions:
whether independently reconstructed components play an operationally matched
causal role, whether their nativegeometryis preserved, and how anobjective
reads them. We separate these questions in pretrained Mamba-1 models from
130M to 2.8B parameters.
At 130M, prospective transport, response-independent specificity, matched neces-
sity, and restoration establish a recurrent-state mechanism on disjoint populations.
At larger scales, a frozen discover-then-confirm protocol selects one of five lo-
cal planes on a discovery cohort, fixes a response-blind geometric control, and
evaluates the pair on a disjoint confirmation cohort. The selected-restored versus
matched-control contrast is positive under each scale’s frozen confirmation, while
a distinct historical 370M residual criterion remains failed.
Coordinate-insensitive comparison of matched response-blind XG2/XG4 popu-
lations yields heterogeneous, intermediate cross-scale similarity (centered linear
CKA: XG20.34–0.74; XG40.39–0.59), with the same qualitative pattern under
cosine-RSM. For the primary frozen native contrasts, downstream task and pre-
trained next-token objectives give opposed five-scale point-estimate sign vectors.
Pair-resampling intervals exclude zero in 8 of 10 scale-by-objective cells, and a
1.4B sensitivity contrast changes the pretrained-LM sign, showing that the aggre-
gate orientation is contrast-conditioned.
Thus a scale-local interventional role can recur without shared plane identity or
exact geometric invariance, while functional readout depends on both objective
and the frozen native contrast. We frame this as a controlled Mamba-1 case study
rather than a universal scaling law.
1 INTRODUCTION
When mechanistic structure is compared across model sizes, “the same mechanism” can refer to
different objects. We distinguishcausal role(what changes under a matched intervention),geo-
metric realization(how that role is represented in native state space), andobjective-conditioned
functional meaning(how a particular objective reads the state). Throughout,recurrencemeans
recurrence of an operationally matched causal role: independently reconstructed scale-local compo-
nents satisfy the same selected-restored versus matched-control intervention contrast on fresh con-
firmation data. It does not mean shared coordinates, principal-plane identity, or semantic homology
across models.
We study five pretrained Mamba-1 models: 130M, 370M, 790M, 1.4B, and 2.8B. We first establish
one recurrent-state mechanism deeply at 130M using prospective transport, response-independent
geometric specificity, matched necessity, and restoration. Larger scales then use a frozen discover-
then-confirm protocol rather than reusing the 130M plane identity.
The first cross-scale result is a separation. A scale-local causal role survives its own frozen matched-
control confirmation at every tested size even though the selected principal-plane rank changes. In
contrast, response-blind alignment geometry shows heterogeneous, intermediate cross-scale con-
servation: centered linear CKA spans0.34–0.74for XG2 and0.39–0.59for XG4, with secondary

The second result concerns functional use. For each scale’s primary frozen native contrast, the

These findings motivate three contributions:
1.Operational causal-role recurrence.Independently reconstructed scale-local compo-
nents survive a common selected-restored versus matched-control criterion on fresh confir-
mations; the recurring object is procedural and interventional, not a fixed plane.
2.Geometric variation with partial conservation.Response-blind CKA and cosine-RSM

2 RELATEDWORK
Mechanistic interpretability of Mamba and state-space models.Mamba replaces attention with
input-dependent selective state-space dynamics, making recurrent state a natural object for mecha-
nistic analysis (Gu & Dao, 2024). Ensign and Garriga-Alonso partially reverse-engineer the indirect-
object-identification circuit in Mamba, identifying a key bottleneck, convolution-mediated token
shifting, and linear storage of name information in the SSM state (Ensign & Garriga-Alonso, 2024).
Wang et al. compare Transformers and Mambas using sparse autoencoders and circuit analysis,
finding substantial feature similarity and structurally analogous induction mechanisms while also
identifying Mamba-specific differences (Wang et al., 2025). More recent work identifies activation-
subspace bottlenecks across several state-space models and tests their causal relevance through in-
terventions and steering (Mohan et al., 2026).
Our question is complementary. Rather than asking whether Mamba contains an interpretable fea-
ture or circuit for a particular benchmark, or whether two architectures implement analogous al-
gorithms, we ask whether acausal role identified inside one Mamba scale recurs after native
geometry is independently reconstructed at larger scales. We then separate that recurrence from
both geometric invariance and downstream functional interpretation.
Representation similarity and cross-model recurrence.Representational-similarity methods
such as centered kernel alignment provide coordinate-insensitive tools for comparing neural rep-
resentations (Kornblith et al., 2019). The Platonic Representation Hypothesis further argues that
representations may become increasingly aligned across models and modalities as capability grows
(Huh et al., 2024). At the same time, recent work shows that representational-similarity conclusions
can depend strongly on objectives and evaluation datasets (Ciernik et al., 2025), and formal work
on linear representations emphasizes that geometric interpretation itself depends on the structure
used to compare representational spaces (Park et al., 2024). Sun and Toneva formalizeinterpre-
tive equivalenceacross neural networks and derive conditions relating representations, circuits, and
algorithmic interpretations (Sun & Toneva, 2026).
Our empirical question is narrower and complementary. We do not require two models to share
a direction, a principal-plane rank, a globally aligned coordinate system, or an interpretation in
the formal equivalence sense. We ask whether independently reconstructed components can sat-
isfy the sameoperational interventional role, and then test whether response-blind geometry and
objective-conditioned readout recur with it.
Causal interventions and functional interpretation.Mechanistic interpretability increasingly
distinguishes correlational localization from causal evidence. Prior work has used interventions and
causal tracing to localize model computations, edit factual associations, and formalize mechanistic
claims using causal abstractions (Dai et al., 2022; Meng et al., 2022; Geiger et al., 2023). Our pro-
tocol follows the same general principle that localization alone is insufficient, but places additional
emphasis on prospective matched controls. At 130M, a localized component must transport to fresh
data, outperform a response-independent geometric control, show selective necessity, and recover
the response under matched restoration before serving as the basis for cross-scale comparison.
Finally, representation and causal role do not uniquely determine how a state is used by a particular
objective. Our setting permits a controlled comparison: within each Mamba scale, the native contrast
and source population are held fixed while its local functional readout is evaluated under either the
structured downstream task objective or the original pretrained next-token objective. We therefore
distinguishobjective-conditioned functional meaningfrom both native geometry and causal-role
recurrence.

3.1 DEEP CAUSAL ESTABLISHMENT ATMAMBA-130M
We first establish the local recurrent-state mechanism in Mamba-130M before asking whether any
part of it recurs at larger scales. Here, acausal rolemeans that manipulating a scale-local native-
state component changes a pre-specified recurrent-state endpoint relative to matched controls, and
that restoring the component recovers that endpoint.
Starting from controlled semantic contrasts, we localized a dominant native-state component, PP3,
and evaluated it through prospective experiments on disjoint XG1 controlled-generator holdouts
(Fig. 2A). PP3 denotes the third principal plane of the independently reconstructed 130M geometry;
its rank label is local to this model and does not denote a globally shared semantic feature.
The localized PP3 effect first transported to an independent 300-pair population. A second holdout
then compared PP3 against PP5, selected independently from response values as a geometric control,
and showed that PP3 retained the stronger effect. Subsequent matched interventions established
both necessity and restoration sufficiency: neutralizing PP3 attenuated the endpoint more strongly
than the control intervention, while restoring the exact PP3 component recovered the endpoint more
strongly than an equal-norm PP5 replacement. All four pre-specified tests supported their respective
hypotheses. Exact test statistics are reported in Appendix A.
The 130M intervention also reached the downstream task computation. Restoring PP3 instead of
the matched PP5 replacement increased the downstream task margin by0.00833on average over
300 paired items (Fig. 2B). This was a small continuous margin effect rather than an accuracy gain;
discrete prediction changes were negligible. The10 −8-scaleQmeans reported for the local causal
tests are in the internal endpoint’s native units and should not be read as downstream behavioral-
effect magnitudes; their very smallp-values reflect consistency relative to paired variation. Across
source pairs, the local task-functional readout tracked the behavioral restoration effect with Pearson
r= 0.618, Spearmanρ= 0.832, and 82.3% sign agreement (Fig. 2C). These associations are
descriptive and were not used to select the mechanism.
The object carried into the scale study is therefore not “PP3” as a fixed semantic plane. It is an
operationally matched, scale-local recurrent-state causal role under a frozen scientific procedure.

We additionally evaluate external transfer on the official A VeriTeC development set (Schlichtkrull

probe. The external evidence therefore supports a bounded conclusion: the native-state intervention
can propagate to continuous task and natural-language decision margins, but the available evidence
does not establish uniform behavioral improvement, scale-invariant external transfer, or benchmark-
level correction.

5 DISCUSSION
The strongest conclusion is procedural: across five Mamba-1 checkpoints, a scale-local compo-
nent selected under a frozen protocol survives a pre-specified matched-control confirmation, despite
changing principal-plane rank. This is recurrence of an operational interventional role, not evidence
that the models contain one coordinate-identical or semantically homologous “same mechanism.”
The 370M aggregate-residual criterion remains a genuine historical failure and is not rescued by
later confirmations.
Response-blind alignment geometry is neither identical nor absent across scales. Centered linear
CKA and cosine-RSM show heterogeneous intermediate conservation, and source-pair resampling
preserves that structure. Because we do not estimate a noise ceiling or cross-model null, the geomet-
ric claim is limited to lack of exact invariance under the stated comparison; it does not quantify how
surprising the observed CKA range should be for independently reconstructed models of different
sizes.
Functional readout is likewise conditional. The primary frozen contrasts have opposed task-versus-
pretrained point-estimate sign vectors, with pair-resampling support in 8 of 10 scale-by-objective
cells, but the 1.4B pretrained-LM sign changes under the common-P3/P5 sensitivity contrast. We
therefore useobjective-conditioned functionalizationonly to mean that a fixed native contrast can be
read differently by different objectives; we do not claim a contrast-invariant or universal scale-level
orientation.
The intervention reaches a continuous task margin at 130M and small external A VeriTeC margins
at 130M/370M, while the 1.4B external negative point estimate misses its prospective gate. These
scope tests do not establish benchmark improvement. More broadly, five checkpoints from one
Mamba-1 family and one controlled mechanism constitute a carefully staged case study, not an
architecture-general scaling law. Comparison with Mamba-2 or Transformer families remains future
work.
Within these bounds, the study provides an empirical separation among three notions that are often
conflated in cross-model mechanistic comparison: recurrence of an operational causal role, conser-
vation of response-blind geometry, and objective-conditioned use of a frozen native contrast.
